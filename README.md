# Grand Horse Mountain

A horse and its rider skiing down a mountain in winter: watched, or ridden. Play it at
**https://horsemountain.klaine.net** (GitHub Pages, from `main`).

It opens on its title over the showcase, and then on the map of the runs: an old map in ink of the mountains about them, as the
world's map is drawn in klaine/dungeons, the hachures heavier the steeper, woods as little trees, rivers, lakes, roads, villages and
the great peaks, and over it, as a ski resort's map has them, the eight runs in the inks of their grades (blue, red, black, each with
a mark of its own), numbered and named, a flag at each top, under a cartouche with the range's name and a compass rose. Beside it,
*Watch the showcase*, *Watch the demo run* or *Watch the orc hunt*; or a run, clicked on the map or in the list, opens its card: its grade, how long it is,
how far it falls, how steep it is on the whole and at its steepest, its profile from top to foot (the pointer over it says how far
down, how high and how steep there), how broad its path is, and the best won on it; and *Ride* it, from its top. (Or the groomed
slope, under the list.) Until then, while it loads, a screen says how far it has got: a bar that fills as its files come in (all
fetched at once, some 6 MB: the slice of the mountain, the map, the figures' clips, the rider's body), and as the run is made, stage
by stage (painting the snow and rock, laying the snow, teaching the rider to ski, dressing the rider and saddling the horse, flying the
demo run, inking the map, lighting the mountain), with what is being done and how much has come under it.

- **The showcase**, the opening's (*Showcase* in the menu too), down the Silent Warden: the horse and its rider (or the skier alone)
  taking a trick off six of its kickers, each coming down facing the way it went (a cork 720, a backflip 360, a 720, a barrel roll
  with a superman whip, a double cork 1080, a double backflip), and passing the sixth by off the piste, out in the deep powder beside it on whichever side no
  stones show and no tree stands; orcs coming up the run all the while (whether or not *Orcs & weapons* is on), met with every arm in
  turn, the sword to the bazooka, changing at the camera's cuts, the flamethrower pouring the whole while it is carried; and at the
  foot the robot arm coming up out of the snow, throwing it back to the top under the parachute, and the showcase again from there.
  Rendered too (in `recordings/`, which git keeps out): its whole, an edit of a minute, one of forty seconds and highlights of
  twenty, at 2 Mbps with sound and without.
- **The demo run** goes down the mountain from beside its highest summit, carving between seven kickers built on the snow and taking a
  trick off each (a 720, a backflip, a backflip 360, a ski jumper's flight, a cork 540 that lands it switch, a switch 540 that brings
  it round again, a superman whip), with turns on the snow to switch and back on the way to the foot; filmed by a cinematic camera
  that cuts from shot to shot, cameras set down by each kicker for its jump.
- **The orc hunt**, once *Orcs & weapons* is on in the menu (*Orc hunt* there, or `O`): a second demo run, the horse
  and its rider, the wildest trick each kicker will give them (a cork 720, a double backflip, a 900 and a switch 900, a superman whip
  backflip 360, a double cork 1080...), and the rider shooting down the orcs coming up the run with a tommy gun as they go.
- **Riding it yourself**, seen from behind: on a phone or a tablet, tipping it steers, from however it is held as each run begins:
  its right edge lowered (as a wheel is turned, or tipped lying flat) to turn right, further to skid; its top tipped away to tuck,
  toward you to stand straight (*Steering* in the menu: *Tilt*, at first, or *Stick*); held still a few seconds a little off level, it
  takes that as level again by itself. The stick at the lower left steers too, and
  takes over while a thumb is on it: to either side to turn (carved, or skidded out at its rim), up to tuck, down to stand straight
  (an iPhone asks, at the first touch, whether the game may read how it is held). With a mouse or a touchpad, the pointer steers from wherever it is over
  the picture, no button held: to either side of the middle to turn (skidded out at the sides of the faint guide drawn about the skier),
  above it to tuck, below to stand straight; it takes the skier once it is brought within the guide, and lets it go, running straight,
  when it leaves the picture. Held down, its button brakes; a right click (a tap with two fingers) turns you round; scrolled or
  pinched, the view comes nearer or goes further off. The arrow keys too, and Shift to brake. You may ride out past the poles, off the marked piste, as far as you like over the land about the run (all of it
  drawn finely, short of its edge; on the groomed slope, anywhere): back between them within thirty seconds, counted down on the picture from the moment you leave them (large over the last five), or you are brought to a stop and
  the run's end (the way back you chose) throws you onto the nearest snow of the marked piste, the run going on from there
  (*Off piste* in the menu, *Allowed* or *Not allowed*, kept in the browser; `?offpiste=0`). Beyond the poles the snow gives way here and there to the mountain's stones, where it is too
  steep for it to lie: the skis grate over them, held back and jarred, and taken onto them fast, over 47 km/h, an edge is caught and
  you are thrown. Off the groomed piste the snow is deep and loose, unlined: you sink into it (less the faster), the tips of your
  skis planing up out of it (a horse carried a little nose up), throw powder up, leave a broad trench behind, and gather speed slowly, some 55 km/h at the most where the piste runs to 85. Off the piste stand trees too: glance
  off one and you go on along it, slowed; run into one, or fly into one, and you are thrown. You steer freely, all the way round, and
  can go back up the run the way you came. The skis are twin tips, their tails turned up as their tips are, and riding switch the
  ski on the outside of a turn goes a little ahead. Each run begins with a push off the poles, and slowed below 10 km/h on the snow (not braking)
  you skate, a stride with each leg in turn (the horse a side at a time), until you are going 25 km/h again; uphill too, or in deep
  snow, pushing the harder, climbing at a walk, so that nowhere leaves you stuck. The kickers are of many sizes, from a lip a metre high to one of three and a half at the end
  of a ramp of thirteen: each as big as the land after it gives room to come down on softly at some 72 km/h. Lined up on one
  faster than it is safe to take (a big one at the foot of a steep stretch, as the Silent Warden's fifth), a little over you absorb
  its lip and fly a little less far; well over, some 11 km/h and more, your skis are thrown across to check your speed just before it,
  *Speed check* said over the picture, before the trick off it is chosen. And taken fast over a
  crest that rounds over sharply, you spring off it into the air as a skier does off a roller (not often: at 70 km/h and more, where
  the snow falls away nearly as fast as you would fall). You make no tricks: line up on a kicker
  and one is done for you, chosen at random among those that should come round in the time that jump will keep you in the air at your
  speed (from a straight air or a grab, through 180s to 1080s, corks, backflips and barrel rolls, rolled over sideways, to double
  backflips and double corks at the wildest), milder or wilder as the upright slider at the lower right says, *Wild* at its top and *Mild* at its foot. Each trick's turns are paced
  to the time the jump gives it, to come round about a third of a second before the snow. Over the slider, *180* (`X`; lit
  while you ride switch) turns you round on the snow, to switch or back, each time the next of a pop 180, a nose butter, a smooth 180 and a tail butter. And over that, *Skate* (held, or `Z`) skates you on, a stride
  with each leg in turn, at any speed: a little more speed, the less the faster you go, none past some 86 km/h. A run ends after a fall (a body sliding on down steep snow dug in after a second and a half, and soon stopped) or at
  the foot of the run, and you are asked how to get back to the top: one of nine ways, or *Random*. Tick *Remember my choice* and
  you are not asked again; *Back to the top* in the menu changes it (or sets it to be asked again). By chance, each way comes once
  in every round of them, in an order shuffled afresh. A huge robot arm rises out of
  the snow, takes the skier up in its claw (a horse and its rider together, the rider put back in the saddle), swings round and throws
  it back up the mountain in a great arch. Or, coming down the last stretch of the run, a whirl of snow rises at its foot and grows as
  you come down to it into a tornado under a dark turning cloud, the light going grey; stopped at the foot it comes over to you,
  ridden into it takes you there and then, and after a fall one forms beside you and comes for you; it carries the skier round and
  up its funnel, tumbling, and flings it out toward the top of the run. Or cracks run out glowing through the snow beside you, and
  the ground blows up, a fireball, smoke and slabs of snow and rock, throwing you up out of it. Or a snowman twenty metres high rises
  out of the snow beside you with a fir pulled up by the roots, and drives you back up the mountain like a golf ball. Whichever,
  over the top of the run a parachute opens and lets the skier down onto its skis there, and the next run begins, in the view it was
  ridden in. Or a flying saucer comes down out of the sky and takes you up in its beam, and carries you back in it, climbing away
  over the mountain between, to hover over the top of the run and let you down its beam onto your skis there; and off it goes up
  into the sky, and the next run begins. Or a shock goes
  through the ground and the land turns inside out about you, folding over in a wave that rolls away across the mountains: where you
  stand is now the height of the land and the peaks are pits. You are turned round to ride the run back down to its top; there (or
  wherever you fall on the way) the land folds back the right way up, and the next run begins. Or a rocket cart comes down beside
  you under a parachute; you get on, its engine lights, and you drive it back up the run, tucked for more push, to the top, where the
  next run begins (fall off, and another comes down). (Neither of the last two on the groomed slope, which has no top to come back
  to.) Or a small high-winged plane comes in low over the snow, a hook trailing on a line under it, and snatches you up as it passes;
  it climbs away over the mountain with you hanging from it, kicking, and lets you go short of the top, so that you fall, your
  parachute opens and lets you down onto your skis there. Or the ground cracks and a tower of ice rises out of it under you, a ski
  jump's in-run of ice and snow falling from its top toward the top of the run; you drop in, crouched as a ski jumper is, take off
  from its table and fly all the way back as a ski jumper flies, laid out over your skis, and your parachute lets you down at the top.
  And choosing another run while you ride one (on the map, `M`), you are taken there the same way: the new run is made while you ride
  on, *Off to* it said over the picture, and then one of the ways that carry you through the air (the one you keep, if it is one, or
  one by chance) takes you to its top where it lies in the world, kilometres off, over the country between; the old run is let go of
  once you are down. The tornado gathers at the foot of the run as you come down to it only when the way back is known as the run begins
  (remembered, or dealt by chance); asked at the end, it forms beside you then. All of it is filmed as a film is, whatever the view;
  Enter, a click, or on a phone a tap, begins the next run at once. Riding wins points, thrown up over the picture where
  they are won: a jump landed is worth what was made of it (each half turn of spin, each flip, a cork, a grab, switch), the more the
  longer in the air, and its length; the most won in one run on each slope is kept in the browser, and shown beside them. With *Orcs & weapons* on in the menu (off at first), the skier alone or the
  horse's rider carries a weapon in place of its poles, and takes up a better one as it brings orcs down, counted over every run
  until the page is opened again: first the sword, a long one for both hands drawn from its back (its scabbard left empty there),
  a cut at a click (or a tap), a second and a half to each, drawn up slowly over a shoulder and down past that side quickly (the side the orcs within reach are on, else the right and
  the left in turn), and you can ride them down too; ten more down, a pistol, held out in both hands on straight arms, a shot a click; fifteen more, a shotgun, put to the shoulder, a spread
  of shot a click; twenty more, a flamethrower, held, a jet of burning fuel thrown some twenty metres that splashes over the snow and
  leaves it burning; twenty-five more, the tommy gun, put to the shoulder, held to fire; thirty more, a lightning gun, held, a bolt every
  0.45 seconds, which leaps from orc to orc, four at the most; thirty-five more, a bazooka, laid on the shoulder, a rocket a click, which blows up all within seven metres of where it strikes, the run's end's
  explosion made small. On a computer each is aimed at the guide's mark, the button firing it (and Shift braking); on a
  phone or a tablet, a finger on the picture fires it there, an orc near the finger aimed at itself, while tilting or the stick
  steers (fewer orcs at a time there). What is carried is said beside the count, and a bar at the left of the picture fills toward the next, how many more at its
  foot, gilt as it is taken up. Orcs come up the
  run in hordes to be brought down, red blood on the snow (none from the burnt or the struck by lightning), how many counted with
  the info; each shot, burnt or struck down a hundred points and a white cross where a round struck, each run down or cut down a
  hundred and fifty, and a double, a triple, for each downed hard on the last.

**The caves.** Halfway down every run, a cave opens beside the piste in a face of rock under a mound of snow: in a bank that rises
beside it where there is one (Silent Warden, Warden's Spur, Balestead), elsewhere in a knoll raised for it near the edge of the run's
snow, the piste drawn in beside it so that the knoll and its camp stand off the run. Two torches at its mouth and a camp before it: a fire in a ring of stones smoking, logs to sit on, a rack, wood
stacked. Ride into the camp and the skier brakes to a stop and takes its skis off, leaving them (and the poles) on the rack; the horse's
rider gets down and leaves the horse standing by the rack. On foot the view is from above, as a dungeon game's is (Diablo, Minecraft
Dungeons), and the controls are its own: W A S D (or the arrows) go up, down, left and right across the picture, running (Shift,
walking); the mouse aims, and a click attacks (with nothing carried, walks to where it is; the right button always walks there, held, after
the pointer); by touch, the stick goes, and a tap attacks there (or walks there); the wheel, or *Distance*, brings the view nearer or
further. Into the mouth and down a tunnel of rough rock to a gate of dressed stone, the snow over it all no longer drawn, the dark about,
and the cave cut away over a height above its floors, its roofs and the tops of its walls, the cut stone capped dark. Behind the gate, a dungeon made afresh each time the page is
opened (the same while it stays open; its seed is in `__slope.cave()`, and `?dungeon=` a given one brings it back), grown as watabou's
One Page Dungeon grows one, by tags dealt with it (`?tags=` names any): its size (*small*, most often, *medium* or *large*: five to
thirteen rooms), its layout (*ordered*, regular rooms straight on from each other; *winding*, long corridors, a chain; *chaotic*, any size,
branching, with ways round; *compact*, rooms close together), its levels (*flat*, or *multi-level*: a stair down, two in a large one),
and what it is (*tomb*: crypts, an ossuary, a shrine, the tomb itself with its great coffin on a dais; *temple*: shrines, libraries,
a pillared hall, the sanctum; *prison*: cells, guardrooms, the warden's room; *vault*: vaults, stores, the hoard). Each room is grown
off an earlier one down a short corridor, its doorway an open arch, a door standing open or a portcullis drawn up, and furnished as
its kind is from the props made for it: barrels, crates, sacks, tables, a desk, stools, benches, bookcases of books, scrolls,
candlesticks and candelabra, altars, statues, coffins, a dais, urns, braziers burning, banners, skulls, bones and skeletons, shackles,
bedrolls, buckets, a rack of spears, heaps of gold, cobwebs. Furthest from the gate, behind a portcullis, the last room and its chest
(5,000 points, once a run). It is lit by its torches, candles and braziers and the light carried in; the place is named as you come in
at the gate, and each room said as you come into it. With *Orcs & weapons* on, what you carry comes in with you, held as on skis on the walk (aimed with the pointer), and orcs come at you in the dungeon: out of rooms some way off and out of sight, two to four at a time, finding their
way to you round the walls, through the doorways and up and down the stairs, striking at you as they come up to you (200 points lost,
and you are thrown back a step); shot, cut, burnt and blown up as on the run, your rounds stopped by the walls. Walk out of the camp, and the skis go back on (the rider back in the saddle) and
you ride on down from there.

*Menu* (or `H`), at the top right, opens everything else: beside the picture on a computer or a phone held sideways, as a sheet from
the foot on a phone held upright, the picture sliding aside so that the skier stays in sight. In it, riding, *Restart run* first, at its
top; then *Play* (watch the demo or ride; watching, pause, its speed and its timeline, which names its parts and can be dragged along;
riding is held while the menu is open), *Slope* (*Map of the runs*, the map the game opens on, or `M`; eight runs down the mountain: *Silent Warden* from beside its highest summit along a crest; *Pale
Crown*, longer, down a spur into the wooded foothills above a bay; *Ashbridge*, short and steep, along the foot of the range
under its rock walls to a lake in a glen; *Carndale*, long and gentle, down a wooded spur in the west to a lake; *Warden's Spur*, from
the summit down its south spur; *Lake Wall*, from a knoll at the head of a long spur, down its broad back and then steeply, with big kickers, to the lake Ashbridge comes to; *Balestead*, down a crest inside
the range; *Arford*, long, gentle and broad, to the shore; or a groomed slope
going on for ever: one run is held at a time, the one left let go of), *Skier* (the horse with its
rider, the horse alone, or a person on skis, an adventurer with a sword on its back; the horse's skis, two long or four short),
*Camera* (*Cinema*, *Behind*, *Beside*, *Above*, or *Free*, turned by hand; `C`, `B`, `S`, `A`, `F`; watching begins in *Cinema* and
riding from behind, and each keeps the one it was last left in; and its distance; from behind on a phone held upright, twice as far
off to begin with) and *Picture* (the snow detailed or plain, the minimap shown or
hidden, how finely the picture is drawn, the blur). Escape or `H` closes it. `M` opens the map of the runs (riding, the ride held while it is open). `R` switches between watching and riding; `Space` pauses, the demo or the ride (which says so).

The info at the top left (what the skier is doing, how fast, how far down, the points and the orcs brought down) is hidden at
first, the picture clear: *Info at the top left* in the menu under *Picture* shows it (`I`; `?info=1`), kept in the browser.

The minimap, at the right under *Menu* (`N` shows or hides it; `?minimap=0`): the whole run small, turned so that the way down it
points up, as the way ahead does in the picture seen from behind; its marked piste pale, its kickers in brass, its top a green dot
and its foot a dark bar; and the skier on it, an arrow the way it goes, gold while off the piste and hollow at the map's edge when
further out than it shows. Light to draw: the run is drawn once for each run, and each frame only the arrow set over it. (None on
the groomed slope, which has no foot.)

In the address: `?nointro` (no title, no map), `?offpiste=0` (kept between the poles), `?skier=person` or `?skier=horse`, `?skis=four`, `#w2-3` (the second run; `#w2-8`, `#w2-10`, `#w2-2`, `#w2-14`, `#w2-17`, `#w2-25`, the third to the eighth), `?land=groomed`, `?snow=plain`, `?orcs=1` (*Orcs & tommy gun* on), `?coat=plain` (the horse's coat as it is drawn on a phone; `?coat=hair`, as on a computer), `?trees=plain` (the snowy firs, as on a phone; `?trees=game`, the game's own trees, as on a computer),
`?dungeon=` (the cave's dungeon from a given seed, rather than a new one), `?tags=` (its tags: `?tags=prison,large,winding`), `?view=behind` (or `beside`, `above`; watching), `?rideView=cinema` (or another; riding), `?menus=1` (the menu open), `?blur=0` to `2`, `?res=1` (or `0.75`, `0.5`; otherwise drawn finer or coarser as
frames come), `?webgl` (WebGL 2 rather than WebGPU). Where the graphics report that they could not draw something as the page opens, it opens
again drawn more simply (the plain snow; then by WebGL 2) and keeps to it in that browser, said in the menu under *Picture* with a way
to try the full picture again; `?safe=0` forgets it, `?safe=1` or `?safe=2` asks for it.

## Working on it

```bash
npm install
```

```bash
npm run dev
```

serves `game.html`, the page the game is written in, with its figures and its slice of the mountain from `figures/` and `slopes/`.

```bash
npm run typecheck
```

```bash
npm run build
```

writes `index.html`: the game as one page, its program and three.js written into it, finding `figures/` and `slopes/` beside it.
GitHub Pages serves it from the top of `main` (with `.nojekyll`, and `CNAME` for the domain), so rebuild it and commit it with any
change to the game.

```bash
npm run skirun -- w2 8 10 --picture runs
```

makes the world `w2` again (in some seconds) and looks for runs down it (in some minutes), lists every run it keeps, best first: where
each starts and ends, how far it falls, how steep it is, how much of it keeps to a crest, the nearest of the world's great peaks and
the nearest village or town to its foot; and writes the slices of those named by their place in that list, `slopes/w2.json` for the
first and `slopes/w2-<n>.json` for the `n`th (here the eighth and the tenth, the slices of *Ashbridge* and *Carndale*, made again as they are). With no runs named it only lists them. With
`--picture`, it also draws the search into that folder, over the whole world and about each run written. A run written is offered
in the game once it is added to the runs in `game/slope.ts` (or at once, with `?world=w2-5` in the address, for the fifth).

```bash
npm run skirun -- w2 --map 1,3,8,10,2,14,17,25
```

writes the map of the runs named (`slopes/w2-map.json`): what the world's ink map needs of the world about them, five kilometres
and more across, on a lattice of sixteen metres (`--cell` another), its rivers and ways thinned to what the map can show; and each
run's line, its profile and what it is like, from its slice. The game draws the map from it as it opens.

## What is here

- `game.html`, `game/slope.ts`: the page and its program (the loading screen, the opening, watching and riding, the demo run's pilot, the tricks given a
  rider, the views, the sky and the light, the picture's blur, grade and resolution); `game/slopeLand.ts`: the land it goes down, the slice of the mountain or the
  groomed slope, its snow, firs, piste poles and the country round about; `game/slopeTrees.ts`: on a computer, the woods of the land
  about the run as the game grows them (its walk over the world's `treesIn` and `growTrees`, in winter, and spruce where it would grow
  oaks and ashes), whole near the eye, more simply further off and painted beyond, grown in blocks about the eye as it goes;
  `game/slopeArm.ts`, the robot arm at the run's end, the parachute, and the throw's flight; `game/slopeStorm.ts`, the tornado,
  `game/slopeBlast.ts`, the blast, `game/slopeSaucer.ts`, the flying saucer, `game/slopeSnowman.ts`, the snowman, `game/slopePlane.ts`, the airplane, `game/slopeIce.ts`, the ice jump, and
  `game/slopeInvert.ts`, the land turned inside out, and `game/slopeCart.ts`, the rocket cart, the run's other ends; `game/slopeGun.ts`, the tommy gun, its
  tracers, flash and sound; `game/slopeArms.ts`, the weapons taken up in turn, the flamethrower's fire and the lightning's bolts; `game/slopeOrcs.ts`, the orcs coming up the run;
  `game/slopeSplatter.ts`, their blood on the snow; `game/slopeDungeon.ts`, the cave on Silent Warden, its camp and the dungeon in it (where it is, the snow levelled and cut for it, the rooms grown, the torches' light worked out); `game/slopeFoot.ts`, on foot there, the person by the packs' walk and jog and the horse's walk and trot made here; `game/slopeProps.ts`, the props the dungeon is furnished with; `game/slopeMap.ts`, the map of the runs, the runs over it, their cards and profiles.
- `src/maps/inkWorld.ts`: the world's ink map, as klaine/dungeons draws it (the map of the runs is a stretch of it).
- `src/figures/`: the figures: the person (`alpine.ts`, posed by a handful of numbers, on the ground and in the air), the horse
  (`horse.ts`, made in code on a skeleton of its own; `horseBody.ts`, `horseSculpt.ts`, how it looks; `horseCoat.ts`, its coat as hair, on a computer), the adventurer's clothes and
  sword (`outfit.ts`), skis and poles (`gear.ts`), a body gone limp (`ragdoll.ts`), posing (`posing.ts`), and the figures' files
  (`library.ts`).
- `src/render/`: the game's materials for snow, rock and the far country (`siteMaterial.ts`), the textures they are painted from
  (`textures.ts`), meshes (`geom/meshBuilder.ts`); the game's trees, grown (`planting.ts`) and drawn as cards in levels
  (`vegetation.ts`); `scene.ts` holds only the two types those name.
- `src/core/`: the slice of the mountain as it is kept and read (`skiSlice.ts`), the map of the runs (`runMap.ts`), and the seeded
  random numbers (`rng.ts`); and, for finding runs (the game takes of it only the little the ink map reads), the generated world (`world.ts` and what it is made with: its land
  `terrain.ts`, `worldLand.ts`, `stream.ts`, its places `site.ts`, `valley.ts`, `highlands.ts`, `castle.ts`, `archipelago.ts`,
  `coastland.ts`, `fit.ts`, its ways `way.ts`, its names `names.ts`) and the runs found down it and sliced (`skiRun.ts`).
- `tools/skiRun.ts`: finds runs down the world and writes their slices and their map (`npm run skirun`); `tools/build.mjs`, the game built.
- `figures/`: the figures, by Quaternius, CC0 (see `figures/LICENSE.txt`): `clips.glb` (the mannequin and the clips), and
  `superhero-male.glb` (the body the adventurer is dressed on).
- `slopes/w2.json`, `slopes/w2-3.json` and six more (`w2-8`, `w2-10`, `w2-2`, `w2-14`, `w2-17`, `w2-25`): the slices of the mountain:
  eight runs found in a generated world, by their place among those its search kept (`npm run skirun`; *Lake Wall* carried further up its
  spur than the search began it, `RAISED` there), and the land about each, a metre apart
  near the run (each fetched only when it is skied, about a megabyte, some 0.6 to 0.7 of it as it comes); `slopes/w2-map.json`, the
  map of them (fetched as the page opens).

## Where it came from

Split from [klaine/dungeons](https://github.com/klaine/dungeons), where it is `lab/slope.html` and grew alongside the game: these
are the files that page needs, as they were at its commit `4137fd8`, with `lab/` as `game/`. The world the runs are found in, and
the finding of them (`src/core/`), and the world's ink map (`src/maps/inkWorld.ts`), are as they are there at its commit `6754077`, so
that the slices made here are those made there;
`tools/skiRun.ts` is this repository's own, writing here and listing every run kept. The whole story of how it was built, asked for step by step, is in
that repository's `docs/03-plan.md` (items 64 to 83). The titles are set in Cinzel and Cormorant Garamond, from Google Fonts (SIL Open
Font License).
